Are Crochet Blankets Safe for Babies to Sleep With?

No — not in the sleep space, for a baby under 12 months. Current infant safe-sleep guidance is that babies sleep alone, on their back, on a firm flat surface, with nothing else in the crib: no blankets, no pillows, no bumpers, no soft toys. A crocheted blanket is loose bedding, and an open-stitch one adds an entanglement risk on top of that.

Yes — everywhere else, supervised. Tummy time, the pram, the play mat, over the car seat, on the sofa, in the photographs, and in the toddler bed later. This is not a small list, and it is where handmade blankets actually live.

This page says the uncomfortable thing plainly because a lot of craft content does not, and because someone is going to make this blanket as a gift and needs to know how to talk about it.

What the guidance actually says

The American Academy of Pediatrics safe-sleep recommendations are the standard reference in the US and are broadly mirrored by the NHS in the UK and by health bodies elsewhere. The relevant points:

  • Babies under 12 months sleep on their back, for every sleep.
  • On a firm, flat, non-inclined surface with a fitted sheet.
  • Alone — the sleep surface should be free of soft objects and loose bedding, including blankets, quilts, pillows, bumper pads and stuffed toys.
  • Room-sharing without bed-sharing is recommended, ideally for at least the first six months.

The reason is suffocation and re-breathing risk. A soft object near a baby’s face can obstruct breathing, and an infant under about four months cannot reliably move away from it.

None of this is a judgement about handmade things. It applies identically to a shop-bought fleece blanket, a quilt from a grandmother, and a $200 designer throw. The blanket being handmade changes nothing about the physics.

What is specific to crochet

Two things make an open-stitch crochet blanket slightly worse than a solid one in the sleep space specifically, and neither is a concern in supervised use:

Holes. Fingers, toes and occasionally a whole hand fit through the gaps in a granny square or a lacy stitch pattern. In supervised use this is a non-event. Unsupervised, it is an entanglement risk, and it is worse with larger gaps and bulkier yarn.

Weight and drape. A super-bulky blanket is heavy. That is lovely on a sofa and not something you want unsupervised near an infant.

If you want to reduce both, work a tight stitch in a lighter yarn — single crochet or half double in weight 3 or 4 — rather than an open granny square in weight 6.

So what do you use for sleep?

A wearable blanket or sleep sack — a sleeved or sleeveless bag the baby wears — is the standard recommendation, because it provides warmth without anything loose in the crib. That is what to buy for the sleeping part. The handmade blanket is for the rest of the day.

Once a baby is over 12 months, the guidance relaxes and a blanket in the bed becomes reasonable. This is when a crib-sized handmade blanket comes into its own, and it is why the crib and toddler sizes are worth making even though they will not be used at newborn stage.

How to give a handmade blanket well

New parents get contradictory advice from everyone. The person who is clear and useful stands out.

Say what it is for on the tag. Something like: “For tummy time, the pram and the sofa — and for the big bed when they’re over one.” It takes one line, it removes an awkward decision, and it signals you know what you are talking about.

Make the stroller size, not the crib size, if you want it used soonest. 30 × 35 inches is the size that gets carried around.

Use washable yarn. A blanket that cannot go in a machine will not be used by anyone with a newborn. See the yarn guide.

Secure everything, or leave it off. No buttons, no beads, no ribbons, no sewn-on eyes, no appliqué that can be pulled free. If you want decoration, crochet it in.

What most craft guides get wrong

They avoid the question. A lot of pattern posts for crib-sized baby blankets simply do not mention safe sleep, which leaves the reader to assume it is fine.

They over-correct in the other direction and imply handmade blankets are dangerous objects. They are not. They are just not bedding for an infant, in exactly the same way a shop-bought blanket is not.

They confuse “safe for a baby” with “safe for a sleeping, unsupervised baby”. Almost everything in the disagreement online comes from these two questions being answered as if they were one.

Frequently asked questions

At what age can a baby have a blanket in bed? Current guidance points to 12 months. Some sources say to keep the sleep space clear until 18 months as a margin. Ask your paediatrician — this page is not medical advice.

Are granny square blankets safe for babies? For supervised use, yes. The open holes make them a poorer choice than a solid stitch for anything unsupervised.

Can a newborn use a crochet blanket at all? Yes, awake and supervised — over the legs in a pram, on a play mat, tucked over a car seat while you are watching. Not in the crib for sleep.

Is a crochet blanket safe in a pram? Yes, with supervision, tucked in below the chest and not near the face. Take it off if the baby falls asleep unattended.

What should I give instead, for sleep? A wearable blanket or sleep sack. Pair it with the handmade blanket as one gift and you have covered both.
