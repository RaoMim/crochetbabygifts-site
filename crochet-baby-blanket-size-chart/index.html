Crochet Baby Blanket Size Chart: Every Size, Newborn to Toddler

A preemie blanket is about 18 × 24 inches. A lovey is 12–17 inches square. A receiving blanket is 30 × 30 inches. A stroller blanket is 30 × 35 inches. A crib blanket is 36 × 52 inches. A toddler blanket is 42 × 52 inches.

Those are the sizes almost every crochet blog will give you. Below is the full chart in both units — and then the part nobody tells you, which is that these numbers are a convention, not a standard, and there is exactly one measurement in the whole list that is fixed by anything official.

The size chart

Blanket type Inches Centimetres Who it’s for
Lovey / security blanket 12 × 12 to 17 × 17 30 × 30 to 43 × 43 Newborn+, for holding rather than covering
Preemie 18 × 24 46 × 61 Hospital NICU sizes; often donated
Receiving blanket 30 × 30 76 × 76 Newborn, swaddling and lap use
Swaddle 40 × 40 102 × 102 Newborn, wrapping — needs drape, not stiffness
Stroller / car seat 30 × 35 76 × 89 0–12 months, out of the house
Crib / baby 36 × 52 91 × 132 Sized against a crib mattress
Toddler 42 × 52 107 × 132 18 months–3 years, toddler bed

If you are making a gift and you do not know which to pick: make the 30 × 35 stroller size. It is big enough to be useful past the newborn weeks, small enough to finish, small enough to throw in a bag, and it is not a size that raises safe-sleep questions the way a full crib blanket does.

The one measurement that is actually fixed

Every size in that table is a community convention that got repeated until it looked official. One is not.

In the United States, a full-size crib mattress is regulated. It must be at least 27¼ inches by 51⅝ inches, and no more than 6 inches thick. That is federal, it is checkable, and it is the reason “crib blanket” settled around 36 × 52 inches: it is the mattress plus a hand’s width of overhang on the long sides and a bit more at the top and bottom.

So when you see crib blanket listed as 45 × 60 on one site and 36 × 52 on another, neither is wrong. They are two different opinions about how much overhang looks right. Pick the one you like. Nothing breaks.

What most size charts get wrong

They present opinion as measurement. There is no governing body for crochet blanket dimensions. The Craft Yarn Council publishes standard yarn weights and hook conversions, and those genuinely are standards. Blanket sizes are not on that list. Any chart that says “the standard size is X” is telling you what most people do, which is useful, but it is not the same claim.

They ignore that gauge decides your real finished size, not your stitch count. Two people can follow the same instructions with the same yarn and finish 4 inches apart. If the size matters — a hospital donation with dimension rules, a blanket that must fit a specific pram — measure your work at 6 inches wide and adjust the starting chain, rather than trusting the count.

They rarely mention safe sleep at all. Which matters more than any of this: see below.

The safe-sleep note this chart needs

A handmade blanket is a lovely gift. It is not bedding for an infant.

Current infant safe-sleep guidance is that babies under 12 months sleep on a firm, flat surface with nothing else in the sleep space — no loose blankets, no pillows, no bumpers, no soft toys. A crocheted blanket is loose bedding, and an open-stitch one adds an entanglement risk on top.

That does not mean don’t make it. It means make it for supervised use: tummy time, the pram, the car seat over the top, the sofa, the play mat, the photographs, and later, the toddler bed. Say so on the gift tag. New parents get contradictory advice from everyone and will appreciate the one person who was clear about it.

Frequently asked questions

Should a crochet baby blanket be square or rectangular? Square for receiving and swaddle sizes, rectangular for stroller and crib. Square is easier to keep even; rectangular tucks better.

How much bigger should I go for a gift? Up one size. Babies outgrow the small sizes in weeks and the stroller and crib sizes get used for years.

What size is best for a first crochet blanket? 30 × 30 inches. Large enough to be a real blanket, small enough that you will actually finish it, and square so a mistake in the width shows up early.

Do preemie blankets have special requirements? Often, yes. Many NICUs specify exact dimensions, machine-washable fibre, and no buttons, ribbons or open lacework. Ask before you start, not after.

Is there an official crochet blanket size standard? No. Yarn weights and hook sizes are standardised. Blanket dimensions are convention.
