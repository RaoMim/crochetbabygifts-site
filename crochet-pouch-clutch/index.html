Crochet Pouch Clutch With a Hidden Frame: Free Pattern in Three Frame Sizes

Crochet a flat rectangle for the base, work the sides straight up from it in single crochet, fold two pleats into the top of each face, then single crochet over the bars of a hidden clasp frame. On a 20 cm frame in 2 mm macrame cord the clutch comes out about 36 cm wide and 22 cm tall laid flat, gathered at the top to the frame. The 25 and 30 cm frames are in the size table.

This is our own design, and it has not yet been test-made from this page. A script checks every row, round and pleat against the frame, and the sizes are estimates worked out from a gauge, so the first thing to check is your own base against the size table.

Pick your frame

Frame Clutch, laid flat Base Foundation chain Rounds Cord
20 cm about 36 × 22 cm (14.25 × 8.75 in) about 24.5 × 10.5 cm 40 ch 30 about 330 g (300 to 400 m)
25 cm about 45 × 28 cm (17.75 × 11 in) about 30 × 13.5 cm 49 ch 38 about 520 g (470 to 620 m)
30 cm about 54 × 33.5 cm (21.25 × 13.25 in) about 35.5 × 16.5 cm 58 ch 45 about 730 g (660 to 880 m)

Hidden clasp frames are sold by their length closed: 20 cm is the most common, then 22 to 25 cm, then 30 cm. The part you crochet over is shorter, so the pattern allows a mouth 3 cm shorter than the length on the packet. Measure yours before you start: if its bar is more than 1 cm longer or shorter than the frame named in the table, use the nearest size.

What you need

Every tool our patterns use is on the Crochet essentials page.

  • 2 mm macrame cord, cotton or polyester, the amount in the size table. Cords of this thickness run 90 to 120 m per 100 g, so check your label.
  • A hidden clasp frame, also sold as a click clack frame, a magic lock or an internal tubular frame, in the length you picked. The kind made of round tubes is the one to crochet over.
  • A 4 mm crochet hook, four locking stitch markers, a tapestry needle with a large eye and scissors.

Gauge and abbreviations

Gauge: about 16 single crochet and 18 rows to 10 cm (4 in) in 2 mm macrame cord on a 4 mm hook. We have not measured this on the clutch yet. Nobody publishes a gauge for this exact cord and hook, so it is a planning figure from the nearest published ones, and every size on this page is worked out from it. Make a small swatch first.

The quick check is the base: after the last base row it should measure the size in the table. If it is more than 1.5 cm off, change your hook by half a size and start again.

US crochet terms throughout. The UK name is in brackets.

  • ch: chain. sl st: slip stitch. st(s): stitch(es).
  • sc: single crochet (UK: double crochet).
  • Row end: the side edge of a row. Work one sc into the edge of each row.
  • Face and gusset: the two wide sides of the clutch are the faces; the two narrow strips between them, above the short ends of the base, are the gussets.
  • 40 (49, 58): the first number is for the 20 cm frame, the ones in brackets for the 25 and 30 cm frames.

The pattern

1. Base

Worked in rows.

  • Foundation: ch 40 (49, 58).
  • Row 1: sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across. [39 (48, 57) sts]
  • Rows 2–19 (2–24, 2–29): ch 1, turn, sc in each st. [39 (48, 57) sts]

Do not fasten off. The base should measure about 24.5 × 10.5 cm (30 × 13.5 cm, 35.5 × 16.5 cm).

2. Body

Work in a continuous spiral without joining, with a marker in the first stitch of each round, moved up as you go. In round 1, also put a marker in the first stitch of each of the four sides, so you can find the faces and gussets later.

  • Rnd 1: sc 39 (48, 57) across the last row, sc 19 (24, 29) down the row ends, sc 39 (48, 57) along the foundation chain, sc 19 (24, 29) up the row ends. [116 (144, 172) sts]
  • Rnds 2–30 (2–38, 2–45): sc in each st. [116 (144, 172) sts]

Sl st in the next 2 sts to smooth the step of the spiral, and fasten off. Move the four corner markers up to the last round, straight above where they were.

3. Pleats

Each face has 39 (48, 57) sts across each face, between its two corner markers. On each face, make two pleats, a quarter of the way in from each corner, both folding towards the centre: fold 6 (6, 7) sts to the inside, so that the stitch before them meets the stitch after them, and pin the fold through the top round. The face now measures the same as the frame’s mouth.

4. Frame

Open the frame. Hold one bar along the inside of the top edge of a face, with its hinges at the corner markers. Join the cord at the first stitch of the face and sc 27 (36, 43) over the bar: put the hook through the top round, through all the layers at a pleat, bring it out on the far side of the bar, and work the sc so it wraps the bar. Work the second face over the other bar the same way.

Leave the 19 (24, 29) sts between the bars free at each side. These are the gussets, and they fold in when the frame shuts. Sl st along their top for a neat edge, and weave in every end.

Cord and time

A 20 cm clutch takes about 330 g of cord. That is scaled by area from the one published 2 mm cord clutch with a weight, Mozy Mory’s 500 g for a bigger bag, so it is an estimate, not weighed.

It is about 4,300 stitches, all single crochet: a project for several evenings rather than one.

If something goes wrong

The bag is much wider than the frame can gather. Your stitches are looser than the gauge. Either go down half a hook size, or fold the extra into the pleats: deeper pleats are fine as long as the two on each face match.

The frame will not shut. The gussets are pulling. Check that the sc over the bar stop at the corner markers and that no gusset stitch is caught on a bar.

The base curls. Work round 1 a little loosely, and do not add stitches at the corners: the base turns up into the sides on its own.

Frequently asked questions

Is this a good first project? It is one stitch all the way, so it suits a confident beginner. The fiddly part is working over the frame, and both videos above show it.

Can I make it in raffia? Yes. Published raffia gauges on 3.5 mm are close to 18 sc per 10 cm, a little tighter than the cord, so the same counts make a slightly smaller clutch. Check the base against the table and choose the frame that fits.

Can I add a lining? Yes. Kristina Virka’s video shows a sewn fabric lining from 15:14. Fit it before the frame goes on.
