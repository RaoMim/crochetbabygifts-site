Handmade Baby Gift Ideas That Aren't a Blanket

Give something that gets used in the first month, something for the six-to-twelve-month gap when the presents have stopped, or something for the parent. Those three slots are where handmade gifts are genuinely scarce, and the blanket everyone defaults to fills none of them.

There is a specific reason the blanket is the weak default, and it is not that it is unoriginal. A loose blanket cannot go in the sleep space for the first twelve months — the safe-sleep guidance is a firm flat surface with nothing soft or loose in it. So the classic handmade gift is one the parent has to put away for a year, at a moment when they are being handed four of them.

The three slots worth aiming at

1. Things that get used up

Washcloths, burp cloths, bibs and changing-mat covers are consumed. They get stained, they get worn, they get replaced — which means yours gets used hard rather than admired and stored. Cotton, plain, a set of two or three rather than one.

This is the least glamorous category on the page and it is the one parents remember, because it arrives in the week when nothing is under control.

2. The six-to-twelve-month gap

Everybody gives newborn sizes. The shower happens before the birth, the presents arrive in the first fortnight, and by month five the baby has outgrown all of it and the gifts have stopped.

A 6–12 month cardigan, a 6–12 month hat, or a toy for a baby who can hold things arrives in an empty space. It is the highest-value timing available to you, it costs nothing extra, and almost nobody does it. If you take one idea from this page, take this one.

Our easy crochet baby hat and the easy crochet baby booties on this site, written out from Craft & Crochet’s video, both include a 6–12 month size.

3. The parent

Wildly underrated. A basket liner, a changing-mat cover, a cup cosy, a shawl for feeding at four in the morning. The baby will not notice any gift you make. The parent will notice this one.

Blankets that are not blankets, and one warning

Several popular “alternatives” are blankets wearing a different name, which is fine as long as you know it.

  • A lovey — a small square with a toy head — is a blanket, but it is the version that becomes a permitted comfort object much sooner, and it is three to five hours rather than thirty.
  • A pram or car-seat blanket is a blanket used correctly, because it goes over a buckled child rather than into a cot.
  • A cocoon or sleep sack is where to be careful. Photography cocoons are props, used under supervision for minutes at a time. They are not sleepwear and not sleep equipment, and they should never be described as either.

The car seat rule most gift guides miss

Nothing bulky goes between the baby and the harness. Padded layers compress in a crash, leaving the straps loose against the child — this is standard car-seat safety guidance and it applies to a thick handmade cardigan exactly as it applies to a winter coat.

The correct way to use a handmade blanket in a car seat is over the top of the buckled harness, tucked around, never underneath and never threaded through the straps.

This affects what to make. A cardigan in a 6–12 month size is a house and pram garment. If you want something for the car, make the blanket that goes over the top.

Choosing between them

A short decision path.

  • Baby already born, gifts already arrived? Make the 6–12 month size of something.
  • Shower in under three weeks? Washcloths or a hat and booties set — finished beats ambitious.
  • First baby? Something for the parent. Everything else in the house is for the baby.
  • Second or third baby? Consumables. They have all the equipment and none of the clean muslins.
  • Want it kept forever? A named or dated keepsake, made in cotton so it survives storage.
  • Winter baby? Hat, mittens, and a blanket for over the car seat.

For presentation and what to write on the tag, see how to present a handmade baby blanket and what to write on a gift tag — both apply to anything on this list, not only blankets.

What most guides get wrong

Half their alternatives are blankets. Lovey, cocoon, swaddle, car-seat blanket, milestone blanket — five names, one object. They are good gifts and they belong on the list, but a list of six blankets is not an answer to “something other than a blanket”.

They never mention the car seat rule, and then recommend a thick handmade snowsuit or a padded cardigan for a winter baby. It is the one safety point in this whole category that people get wrong, because it is counter-intuitive.

They size everything for a newborn. The newborn window is six weeks long and it is the most over-supplied period of a child’s life.

They ignore the parent entirely, which is strange, given that the parent is the person opening the gift and the only one who will remember it.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most useful handmade baby gift? Cotton washcloths or burp cloths, in a set. Least impressive to give, most used.

Is a crochet toy safe for a newborn? Yes with embroidered features, no attached parts and double-sewn seams. Not in the sleep space in the first year. Details in the safety checklist.

What size should I make if the baby is not born yet? 6–12 months. It arrives when nothing else does, and a slightly early gift gets put in a drawer for three months rather than never worn.

What should I make for a second baby? Things that get used up, and things in the size the older child has outgrown. They have the equipment; they do not have clean bibs.

Can I give a blanket at all? Of course — it is a lovely gift and it is used from about a year old, and immediately in the pram and over a car seat. Just say on the tag what it is for, so nobody has to guess.
